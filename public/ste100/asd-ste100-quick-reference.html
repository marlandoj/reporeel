This is a practical subset of the 53 writing rules in ASD-STE100 Issue 9, paraphrased for software documentation. The check is mechanical: it proves what a program can prove, not what a judge would decide. Full compliance needs the official dictionary, a free download from asd-ste100.org. Section 1 — Words

RuleInstructionChecked
1.1Use only approved words, technical nouns, or technical verbs.mechanical
1.2Use an approved word only as its listed part of speech.judgement
1.3Use an approved word only with its approved meaning.judgement
1.4Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives.mechanical
1.5You can use domain words as technical nouns, for example webhook or commit.judgement
1.6Use an unapproved word only when it is a technical noun or part of one.judgement
1.7Do not use technical nouns as verbs.judgement
1.8Use the technical nouns of your project or industry.judgement
1.9When you pick a technical noun, pick a short and clear one.judgement
1.10No regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns.judgement
1.11One item, one name. Do not call it config here and settings there.mechanical
1.12You can use domain verbs as technical verbs, for example deploy or merge.judgement
1.13Do not use technical verbs as nouns.judgement
1.14Use American English spelling.mechanical

Section 2 — Multi-word nouns

RuleInstructionChecked
2.1Write multi-word nouns of three words or fewer.mechanical
2.2Write a long technical noun in full once, then give a short form.judgement

Section 3 — Verbs

RuleInstructionChecked
3.1Use only the verb forms that the dictionary gives.judgement
3.2Use only the infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, and participle as adjective.judgement
3.3Use the past participle only as an adjective, for example the cached response.judgement
3.4No auxiliary verbs for complex constructions. No present perfect, no is to be installed.mechanical
3.5Use an -ing form only as a technical noun or inside one, never as a verb.mechanical
3.6Active voice. Passive is legal only when the agent is unknown.mechanical
3.7Describe an action with a verb, not a noun.mechanical

Section 4 — Sentences

RuleInstructionChecked
4.1Write short and clear sentences.judgement
4.2Do not omit words or use contractions to shorten sentences. Keep articles, keep that.mechanical
4.3Use a vertical list for complex text.mechanical
4.4Use connecting words between sentences on related topics.mechanical
4.5Put an article or a demonstrative adjective before the noun where applicable.mechanical

Section 5 — Procedural writing

RuleInstructionChecked
5.1Maximum 20 words per sentence. Warnings and cautions included.mechanical
5.2One instruction per sentence, unless two actions happen at the same time.mechanical
5.3Write instructions in the imperative.mechanical
5.4Put a required condition before the command, divided by a comma.mechanical
5.5Notes give information, never instructions. Notes get the 25-word limit.mechanical

Section 6 — Descriptive writing

RuleInstructionChecked
6.1Give information gradually, one subject per sentence.judgement
6.2Use key words and phrases to give the text a logical structure.judgement
6.3Maximum 25 words per sentence.mechanical
6.4Group related information in paragraphs.judgement
6.5One topic per paragraph.judgement
6.6Maximum six sentences per paragraph.mechanical

Section 7 — Safety instructions

RuleInstructionChecked
7.1Use a word that shows the risk level, for example WARNING or CAUTION.judgement
7.2Start with a clear command or condition.judgement
7.3Then give the risk or the possible result.judgement

Section 8 — Punctuation and word count

RuleInstructionChecked
8.1All standard punctuation is legal except the semicolon. Write two sentences instead.mechanical
8.2Use hyphens to connect words that act as one unit.judgement
8.3Parentheses are legal for references, item numbers, abbreviations, plurals, explanations, and alternatives.judgement
8.4In a vertical list, the lead-in colon ends a sentence for word count.judgement
8.5Text inside parentheses counts as one word.judgement
8.6Count as one word each: numbers, numbers with units, abbreviations, identifiers, quoted text, titles, labels, and proper nouns.mechanical
8.7A hyphenated word counts as one word.judgement

Section 9 — Writing practices

RuleInstructionChecked
9.1When a word-for-word replacement does not work, restructure the sentence.mechanical
9.2Use each approved word with its approved meaning and part of speech.judgement
9.3Do not build phrasal verbs. Write decrease, not go down.mechanical
9.4Keep one consistent style and terminology through the whole document.mechanical
